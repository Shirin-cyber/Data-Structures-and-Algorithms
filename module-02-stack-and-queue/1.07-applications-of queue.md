# 2.7 Applications of Queue

A queue is used wherever things have to be handled **in the order they arrive** (First In First Out).

- Manage **resource sharing** such as **CPU scheduling** and **disk scheduling**
- Handling of **interrupts** in real time
- **Call waiting systems** in call centers

---

## Manage resource sharing

When many processes want the same resource (like the CPU or the disk), they can't all use it at once. So they **wait in a queue** and get their turn in order.

- **CPU scheduling:** processes wait in a ready queue until the CPU is free for them.
- **Disk scheduling:** disk requests are kept in a queue and served one after another.

This keeps the sharing fair, because whoever asked first gets served first.

## Handling of interrupts in real time

In real-time systems, devices like the keyboard send interrupts to the computer. Many can arrive close together, so they're placed in a queue and handled **in the order they came**, without losing any of them.

## Call waiting systems in call centers

When all the agents are busy, new callers are placed in a queue. The call that came in **first** is answered **first** when an agent becomes free.

---

## Other examples 

- **Printer queue:** print jobs are printed in the order they were sent.
- **Breadth-first traversal** of trees and graphs uses a queue.
- **Buffers:** things like the keyboard buffer and video streaming use queues to hold data until it's processed.
- **Ticket booking systems:** requests are served in order of arrival.

---

## Quick Revision

| Application | How the queue helps |
|-------------|---------------------|

| **Interrupts in real time** | Interrupts are handled in the order they arrive |
| **Call waiting systems** | Callers are answered in the order they called |
